## Part 1 — Style survey of public anime Roblox games

### Anime Adventures (tower defense)
- Proportions: units are standard Roblox-style avatar bodies (R15-like, blocky limbs) with large custom mesh hair
  and accessories. They are not true chibi. The head reads big because the hair mesh is oversized.
- Poly level: low-mid. Body is simple boxes. Detail sits in hair, weapons and VFX.
- Shading: mostly flat or lightly textured clothing (2D shirt/pants textures) under default Roblox lighting.
- Distance read: hair silhouette plus hair colour carry identity from the top-down tower-defense camera.
- Names: one-letter or vowel swaps plus a form tag. Examples: "Noruto (Six Tails)", "Itochi (Susanoo)",
  "Goko (Ultra)", "Golden Freezo (Emperor)", "Luffo (Gear IV)", "Usoap (God)", "Vegita (Majin II)". Some lists also
  show unaltered names (Naruto, Alucard), so the practice is inconsistent.
- URL: https://www.pockettactics.com/anime-adventures/tier-list

### Anime Last Stand (tower defense)
- Proportions: R15-style bodies with custom hair and outfit meshes. Proportions are closer to normal Roblox.
- Poly level: low-mid, heavy VFX.
- Distance read: same as Anime Adventures. Hair and colour block are the main cues.
- Names: syllable swaps ("Gogata", "Vagata", "Kohan", "Zuffy", "Roro") or descriptive titles
  ("Flame Alchemist", "Black Swordsman", "Strongest Sorcerer", "Curse King", "Captain Commander").
- URL: https://mobilematters.gg/guides/roblox/anime-last-stand-units-all-units

### Anime Defenders (tower defense)
- Proportions: small units with large heads relative to the body. This game is the nearest to chibi in the survey.
- Poly level: low. Units must read at tower-defense camera distance.
- Shading: bright saturated flat colour. Strong outline-like contrast between hair and outfit.
- Names: almost fully descriptive titles, no character names. Examples: "Ant King", "Skull Warrior", "Esper",
  "Prince of Curses", "Elf Wizardess", "Pink Rockstar", "Ice-Admiral". This is the safest naming pattern.
- URL: https://www.ginx.tv/en/?p=64063 , https://www.gameskinny.com/?p=195697

### Anime Champions Simulator (clicker plus pets)
- Proportions: "champions" follow the player like pets. They are small, with big heads and short limbs.
- Shading: flat toon colours with rarity glow or aura VFX as the status signal.
- Distance read: hair colour plus one big accessory. Rarity glow separates units in crowded lobbies.
- Names: the roster draws on Dragon Ball, JoJo and My Hero Academia (Folha Vitoria). The fetcher found no public
  name list to verify the naming pattern.
- URL: https://progameguides.com/roblox/anime-champions-simulator-roblox-trello-discord-wiki-link/ ,
  https://www.folhavitoria.com.br/games/os-melhores-jogos-roblox-em-2025/

### Anime Fighters Simulator (clicker plus pets, by Sulley, released 2021-01-26)
- Proportions: "fighters" are pet-sized followers with big heads, so they read as chibi pets.
- Poly level: low. Many fighters are on screen at once.
- Names: tweaked names. The fetcher found no verifiable list.
- URL: https://gamerempire.net/anime-fighters-simulator-roblox-characters-guide/ , https://rolearn.dev/games/2324662457/

### Others
- Anime Dimensions Simulator: full-size player-controlled characters with custom meshes and dungeon-camera reads.
  It has its own tier lists (https://Pocketgamer.com/roblox/anime-dimensions-simulator-tier-list/).
- Anime Fighting Simulator (Endless or X) is a different game from Anime Fighters Simulator. It uses
  "champions" with similar pet-style followers (https://www.pockettactics.com/anime-fighting-simulator-endless-champions).

### Legal backdrop
- A "parody" label alone does not exempt a game from copyright. Revenue-earning Roblox games have been taken down or
  forced to rebrand (https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Copyright_and_trademark_infringement).
- Other genres moved to parody names once a game grew popular, for example car brands
  (https://devforum.roblox.com/t/are-there-rules-for-using-names-of-real-firearms-in-games/3164347).

### Takeaways for our procedural chibi style
- Hair is the identity. Every surveyed game uses an oversized hair mesh as the main recognition cue. Spend the
  poly budget on hair presets (spike count, length, fringe) and keep the body shared and simple.
- Use one dominant outfit colour plus one accent per character. At small size a character reads as hair colour over
  outfit colour. Colour blocks matter more than detail.
- Use one big accessory at most: hat, sword, staff, cloak or scarf. Scale it up 1.2-1.5x for chibi. Small items
  such as earrings, rings and badges vanish at distance, so drop them or exaggerate them.
- Use flat toon shading. Give each material one base colour, plus an optional darker band shade or inverted-hull
  outline. No textures, so recolouring stays procedural.
- Big head (~1/3 of height) with large simple eyes. Eye colour is a secondary cue only, because eyes are tiny at
  run-camera distance.
- Marks: altered symbols must be a different shape class, for example a leaf spiral becomes a diamond-bar or a
  cloud becomes a crescent. Never use a recoloured or rotated original. Anime Defenders' title-only naming shows
  that recognition survives without marks.
- Name pattern: our tweaked names match the common vowel/syllable-swap convention seen in Anime Adventures and Anime
  Last Stand. Put the "based on" text only in docs, never in-game.
- Give rivals or rarity tiers an aura or rim-light VFX instead of extra mesh detail, as Anime Champions Simulator does.
